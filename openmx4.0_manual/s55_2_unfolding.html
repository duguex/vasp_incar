55.2 Unfolding of band structures

In this subsection, we explain how a band structure obtained by the supercell method is unfolded into the Brillouin zone of a reference unit cell. As an example, we again consider SiC with a perfect two-dimensional honeycomb structure. However, here we consider a calculation cell expanded to a (2×2) supercell as follows.

The SCF calculation for the supercell of two-dimensional SiC is performed as follows.

  % mpirun -np 16 openmx SiC_C_NSP_P.dat > sic_c_nsp_p.std &

The input file SiC_C_NSP_P.dat is stored in work/unfolding_example. After the SCF calculation is finished, the following files required for the unfolding calculation are generated.

  sic_c_nsp_p.unfold_totup
  sic_c_nsp_p.unfold_orbup
  sic_c_nsp_p.unfold_plotexample

By plotting sic_c_nsp_p.unfold_totup together with the band structure obtained for the primitive cell, one can obtain a figure similar to Fig. 75(a). It can be confirmed that the bands unfolded from the supercell completely reproduce those of the primitive cell.

To perform band unfolding, in addition to the keywords introduced in the previous subsection, the following keywords must be specified.

    0.0000  0.0000000000  10.000
  Unfolding.ReferenceVectors>

  <Unfolding.Map
   1 1
   2 1
   3 1
   4 1
   5 2
   6 2
   7 2
   8 2

  10 3
  11 3
  12 3
  Unfolding.Map>

The meanings of these keywords are explained below.

  • •

    Unfolding.ReferenceVectors

    Unfolding.ReferenceVectors defines the unit cell used as the reference when performing band unfolding according to Eq. (24) of Ref. [155]. In the above example, the primitive cell is taken as the reference cell. The input format is the same as that of Atoms.UnitVectors, and the a-, b-, and c-axes should be given on the first, second, and third lines, respectively. The unit of the reference cell is the same as that used for the supercell, namely that specified by Atoms.UnitVectors.Unit. In Section 55.1, Unfolding.ReferenceVectors was not specified. In such a case, Atoms.UnitVectors is automatically used as Unfolding.ReferenceVectors.

  • •

    Unfolding.Map

    Unfolding.Map specifies how atoms in the supercell are mapped onto atoms in the reference cell. In the first column, write the serial numbers of the atoms given in Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates, and in the second column, write the serial numbers of the groups to which the atoms belong. In the above example, atoms 1 to 4 belong to group 1, atoms 5 to 8 belong to group 2, and atoms 9 to 12 belong to group 3. Based on the information given in Unfolding.Map, the relabeling of each atom is performed according to Eq. (17) of Ref. [155]. The group identifiers only need to be positive integers; they do not have to start from 1 and do not have to be in ascending order.

As a further example, we next consider the case where an Si vacancy is introduced into a (2×2) supercell of two-dimensional honeycomb SiC. First, perform the SCF calculation as follows.

  % mpirun -np 16 openmx SiC_C_SP_V.dat > sic_c_sp_v.std &

The input file SiC_C_SP_V.dat is stored in the work/unfolding_example directory. If one Si atom is removed from the (2×2) supercell, the input file becomes as follows.

After the SCF calculation is finished, the following files related to the unfolding method are generated.

  sic_c_sp_v.unfold_totup
  sic_c_sp_v.unfold_totdn
  sic_c_sp_v.unfold_orbup
  sic_c_sp_v.unfold_orbdn
  sic_c_sp_v.unfold_plotexample

By plotting sic_c_sp_v.unfold_totup and sic_c_sp_v.unfold_totdn with gnuplot, together with the band structure obtained for the primitive cell, one can obtain Fig. 75(b). From the unfolded spectral weights, one can see that the characteristic features of the perfect system are retained even after introducing the defect. It can also be seen that the chemical potential is shifted substantially to higher energy and that spin polarization appears due to the dangling bonds of the carbon atoms. As these analyses show, the band unfolding method can be used to evaluate how the original band structure is affected by the introduction of imperfections.

When an impurity is introduced instead of a defect, the calculation can be performed simply by assigning an identifier to the impurity. If an impurity is introduced as the 13th atom in the SiC (2×2) supercell, the relabeling rule is defined as follows.

  <Unfolding.Map
    1 1
    2 1
    3 1
    4 1
    5 2
    6 2
    7 2
    8 2
    9 3
   10 3
   11 3
   12 3
   13 4
  Unfolding.Map>

Even when multiple impurities are present, the mapping rule can be defined in the same manner.
