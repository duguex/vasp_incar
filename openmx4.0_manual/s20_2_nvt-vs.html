20.2 NVT molecular dynamics by velocity scaling (NVT_VS)

By specifying the keyword MD.Type as NVT_VS, an NVT-ensemble molecular dynamics simulation by the velocity-scaling method [31] can be performed.

    MD.Type          NVT_VS    # NOMD|Opt|NVE|NVT_VS|NVT_VS2|NVT_NH

In this NVT molecular dynamics, the temperature of atomic motion can be controlled by the following format.

    <MD.TempControl
      3
      100   2  1000.0  0.0
      400  10   700.0  0.4
      700  40   500.0  0.7
    MD.TempControl>

The first line of this format is <MD.TempControl, and the last line is MD.TempControl>. The initial 3 is the number of lines required for temperature control. In this case, the temperature is specified in the following three lines, and the number in the first column represents the MD step, while the number in the second column gives the interval of MD steps at which velocity scaling is applied. In this example, velocity scaling is applied every 2 steps up to MD step 100, every 10 steps from MD step 100 to 400, and every 40 steps from MD step 400 to 700. The third and fourth columns represent the target temperature Tgiven (K) and the scaling parameter α in each interval, respectively. The temperature within each interval is given by linear interpolation. The velocities are scaled using the following equations.

s=Tgiven+(Tcalc−Tgiven)∗αTcalc
𝐯i′=𝐯i×s

Here, Tgiven and Tcalc are the target temperature and the temperature calculated from the atomic motion, respectively. In the NVT_VS method, the temperature is calculated using the velocities of all atoms. In contrast, in NVT_VS2, a local temperature is evaluated from the velocity of each atom, and the velocity scaling is performed on the basis of that local temperature. After the final MD step specified by MD.TempControl is reached, the NVT ensemble is switched to the NVE ensemble. The numerical values calculated at each MD step are recorded in the output file System.Name.ene (System.Name means System.Name). They are described in detail in the file iterout_md.c in the source directory, but some of them are listed below.

         1:    MD step
         2:    MD time (fs)
        14:    kinetic energy of nuclear motion, Ukc (Hartree)
        15:    DFT total energy, Utot (Hartree)
        16:    Utot + Ukc (Hartree)
        17:    Fermi energy (Hartree)
        18:    Given temperature for nuclear motion (K)
        19:    Calculated temperature for nuclear motion (K)
        22:    Nose-Hoover Hamiltonian (Hartree)

In the output file *.ene, the first column corresponds to the MD step, the second column to the MD time, and so on. As an example, the result of an MD calculation of a glycine molecule by velocity scaling is shown in Fig. 13 (a).

It can be seen that the molecular temperature oscillates around the target temperature. For visualization of molecular dynamics simulations, OpenMX Viewer [165, 164] and XCrySDen [116] can be used. Using these software packages, the output file System.Name.md can be readily visualized as an animation.
