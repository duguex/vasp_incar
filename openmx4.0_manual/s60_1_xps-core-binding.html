60.1 XPS core-level overview

Within the framework of density functional theory, the absolute binding energies of core levels in metals and insulators can be calculated. A general method for calculating absolute binding energies of core levels has been implemented based on a penalty functional and the exact Coulomb cutoff method [97]. The resulting absolute binding energies can be directly compared with the binding energies measured by X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS). In this method, the spurious interaction between core holes in neighboring supercells is avoided by the exact Coulomb cutoff method. On the other hand, the variational penalty functional method makes it possible to treat chemical shifts, spin-orbit interaction, and multiplet splitting due to exchange interaction on an equal footing. For eight cases including metals, semiconductors, and insulators, the calculated absolute binding energies of core electrons show a mean absolute error of 0.4 eV (the mean relative error is 0.16%) compared with experimental values, demonstrating that this is a generally applicable computational method [97].

As schematically illustrated in Fig. 91, by considering the law of energy conservation in the excitation process of an XPS measurement, the absolute binding energies of core levels in gaseous and bulk systems are respectively given by the following equations [97].

Eb(gas) = Ef(0)⁢(N−1)−Ei(0)⁢(N), (65)
Eb(bulk) = Ef(0)⁢(N−1)−Ei(0)⁢(N)+μ0, (66)

where Ei(0)⁢(N) and Ef(0)⁢(N−1) are, respectively, the DFT total energies of the initial state of the N-electron system and the final state of the (N−1)-electron system. Here, μ0 is the chemical potential obtained from the initial-state calculation. Ef(0)⁢(N−1) is evaluated by the method proposed in Ref. [97]. On the other hand, Ei(0)⁢(N) is calculated by an ordinary band calculation.

Equation (66) is a general expression valid for both gapped systems and metals, but for metals it can be transformed by using Janak’s theorem [99] as follows.

Eb(metal)=Ef(0)⁢(N)−Ei(0)⁢(N), (67)

In the final-state calculation, the total-energy calculation for the (N−1)-electron system can be replaced by that for the N-electron system. This expression is consistent with an intuitive understanding of metallic systems. That is, in a metal, the final state after core-hole creation can be understood as a state in which complete screening has taken place.
