14.1 Convergence

Computational accuracy and computational cost also depend on the cutoff energy used in numerical integration and in solving the Poisson equation [45]. The cutoff energy is controlled by the keyword scf.energycutoff. Figure 6 shows the convergence behavior of the total energy of a methane molecule with respect to the cutoff energy. The input file used here is Methane.dat, which was used in Section 8. Since the cutoff energy is for numerical integration rather than for basis functions, the total energy does not converge from the high-energy side as the cutoff energy is increased, unlike in plane-wave basis systems. In most cases, a cutoff energy of 150 to 200 Ryd is an optimal choice. However, it should also be noted that there are subtle problems that require a cutoff energy higher than 300 Ryd. Calculations of very flat potential surfaces near a minimum, and calculations of small energy differences between different spin orders, may be such subtle problems.

Table 3 shows the dependence of the structural parameters and dipole moment of a water molecule on the cutoff energy. The input file is H2O.dat in the work directory. The results are found to be converged at about 90 Ryd. Although the required cutoff energy depends on the elements, converged solutions will be obtained around 200 Ryd in many cases. However, we recommend that you check how the physical properties of your target system depend on the cutoff energy. For the other cutoff energy, 1DFFT.EnergyCutoff, the default value 3600 (Ryd) is usually used, and this value is sufficiently converged. In addition, its computational cost is small compared with that of the other parts of the calculation.
