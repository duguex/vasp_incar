19.1 Variable-cell optimization

In OpenMX Ver. 4.0, variable-cell geometry optimization can be used both with and without constraints. The relevant keywords are as follows.

   MD.Type                     RFC5       # OptC1|OptC2|OptC3|OptC4|OptC5
                                          # OptC6|OptC7|RFC5|RFC6|RFC7
   MD.Opt.DIIS.History          3         # default=3
   MD.Opt.StartDIIS             5         # default=5
   MD.Opt.EveryDIIS            200        # default=200
   MD.maxIter                  100        # default=1
   MD.Opt.criterion          1.0e-4       # default=0.0003 (Hartree/Bohr)

Since the above keywords are exactly the same as those in Section 18, variable-cell geometry optimization can be controlled in the same manner as conventional geometry optimization. Variable-cell geometry optimization is available only for collinear calculations including the plus U method. However, vdW interactions by the DFT-D2 and DFT-D3 methods are not supported. Variable-cell geometry optimization is specified by MD.Type, and the following options are available.

  • •

    OptC1

    Optimization is performed without constraining the lattice vectors, while keeping the initial internal coordinates fixed. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    OptC2

    Optimization is performed while keeping the initial internal coordinates fixed and fixing the angles between lattice vectors to their initial values. Therefore, only the lengths of the lattice vectors are optimized. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    OptC3

    Optimization is performed while keeping the initial internal coordinates fixed, fixing the angles between lattice vectors to their initial values, and maintaining the lengths of the lattice vectors equal to one another: |𝐚1|=|𝐚2|=|𝐚3|. Therefore, only the lengths of the lattice vectors are optimized. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    OptC4

    Optimization is performed while keeping the initial internal coordinates fixed, fixing the angles between lattice vectors to their initial values, and optimizing the lengths of the lattice vectors under the following constraint: |𝐚1|=|𝐚2|≠|𝐚3|. Therefore, only the lengths of the lattice vectors are optimized. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    OptC5

    The lattice vectors and internal coordinates are optimized simultaneously without constraints. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    OptC6

    The lattice vectors and internal coordinates are optimized simultaneously while keeping lattice vector 𝐚3 fixed. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    OptC7

    The lattice vectors and internal coordinates are optimized simultaneously while keeping lattice vectors 𝐚2 and 𝐚3 fixed. The optimization is carried out by the steepest descent method.

  • •

    RFC5

    The lattice vectors and internal coordinates are optimized simultaneously without constraints. In this option, the internal coordinates are optimized by a hybrid scheme of the RF method [67] and the DIIS method [65], together with an update of the Hessian matrix using the BFGS method [68]. The initial Hessian matrix is specified as iden or Schlegel by MD.Opt.Init.Hessian, and by default Schlegel is used (see Section 18 for details). Also, by combining RFC5 with MD.Fixed.Cell.Vectors, it is possible to impose the same constraints as in RFC6 and RFC7 below. Since arbitrary constraints can be imposed, the use of the combination of RFC5 and MD.Fixed.Cell.Vectors (Section 19.3) is recommended. In addition, RFC5 has been used in variable-cell optimization calculations for a large number of crystal systems [112].

  • •

    RFC6

    The lattice vectors and internal coordinates are optimized simultaneously while keeping lattice vector 𝐚3 fixed. The optimization is carried out by the same method as RFC5. Note that, by combining RFC5 with MD.Fixed.Cell.Vectors, it is possible to impose the same constraint conditions as RFC6.

  • •

    RFC7

    The lattice vectors and internal coordinates are optimized simultaneously while keeping lattice vectors 𝐚2 and 𝐚3 fixed. The optimization is carried out by the same method as RFC5. Note that, by combining RFC5 with MD.Fixed.Cell.Vectors, it is possible to impose the same constraint conditions as RFC7.

Select one of the above options according to your purpose. In the future, constraint conditions other than those above are also planned to be implemented.

As an example of variable-cell geometry optimization, simultaneous optimization of the lattice vectors and internal coordinates of the diamond structure is shown below. The calculation is performed by the following command.

     % mpirun -np 16 openmx Cdia-RFC5.dat > Cdia-RFC5.std &

Since the input file Cdia-RFC5.dat is included in the work/cellopt_example directory, the same calculation can be performed. In this example, the initial structure is distorted as follows.
