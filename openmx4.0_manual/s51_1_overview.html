51.1 NEB overview

One of the representative methods for finding the minimum energy path (MEP) connecting two stable structures in geometrical phase space is the NEB method based on Ref. [147]. In the NEB method, multiple images are placed between the reactant and product, and an elastic band is introduced by connecting them with virtual springs. The whole band is then converged to the MEP by optimizing the images while properly projecting the forces acting on each image.

We first summarize the key points of the methodology. Let the coordinates of image i be 𝐑i, and let the potential energy be Vi=V⁢(𝐑i). In the NEB method, the force acting on each image is decomposed as

𝐅iNEB=𝐅i⟂+𝐅is,∥ (59)

Here, the perpendicular component of the true force 𝐅i⟂ is given, according to Eq. (4) of Ref. [147], by

𝐅i⟂=−∇V⁢(𝐑i)+[∇V⁢(𝐑i)⋅τ^i]⁢τ^i (60)

where τ^i is the unit tangent vector along the path. On the other hand, the tangential component of the spring force 𝐅is,∥ is given, according to Eq. (12) of Ref. [147], by

𝐅is,∥=k⁢(|𝐑i+1−𝐑i|−|𝐑i−𝐑i−1|)⁢τ^i (61)

where k is the spring constant.

The meaning of Eqs. (59)–(61) is clear. For the true force, only the component perpendicular to the path is used so that each image approaches the MEP. On the other hand, for the spring force, only the component parallel to the path is used so that the images are distributed almost uniformly along the path. This projection is essential. Without it, in the simple elastic band method, the spring force tends to shortcut a curved MEP, causing corner-cutting, and images in high-energy regions tend to slide down toward minima, causing sliding-down [147].

Next, we describe the definition of the tangent vector. In the improved NEB method of Henkelman and Jónsson, the tangent is not defined by a simple central difference, but by using the energies of neighboring images. According to Eqs. (8)–(11) of Ref. [147],

τi={τi+=𝐑i+1−𝐑i(Vi+1>Vi>Vi−1)τi−=𝐑i−𝐑i−1(Vi+1<Vi<Vi−1) (62)

and when i is close to a maximum or minimum,

τi={τi+⁢Δ⁢Vimax+τi−⁢Δ⁢Vimin(Vi+1>Vi−1)τi+⁢Δ⁢Vimin+τi−⁢Δ⁢Vimax(Vi+1<Vi−1) (63)

is used. Here,

Δ⁢Vimax=max⁡(|Vi+1−Vi|,|Vi−1−Vi|),Δ⁢Vimin=min⁡(|Vi+1−Vi|,|Vi−1−Vi|) (64)

Finally, it is normalized as τ^i=τi/|τi|. This definition is important for suppressing the formation of kinks, which are problematic in the conventional method, and for achieving more stable convergence to the MEP [147].

In OpenMX Ver. 4.0, the NEB method is implemented based on the methodology described above. The main features of the implementation are summarized below.

  • •

    Calculation of tangents based on Eqs. (8)–(11) of Ref. [147]

  • •

    Calculation of the perpendicular component of the true force based on Eq. (4) of Ref. [147]

  • •

    Calculation of the tangential component of the spring force based on Eq. (12) of Ref. [147]

  • •

    Hybrid optimization method combining DIIS and BFGS

  • •

    Usable in almost the same manner as structural optimization

  • •

    Support for hybrid MPI/OpenMP parallelization

  • •

    Support for both a linearly interpolated initial path and a user-defined initial path
