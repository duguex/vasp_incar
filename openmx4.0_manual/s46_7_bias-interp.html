46.7 Interpolation method for bias-voltage effects

Performing SCF calculations for each bias voltage in a large-scale system is very time consuming. The interpolation method for bias-voltage effects can reduce the computational cost of NEGF calculations. The interpolation is carried out by the following procedure. (i) Perform SCF calculations for two or three bias voltages selected from the target bias-voltage range. (ii) When calculating the transmission and current, linearly interpolate the Hamiltonian block elements Hσ,C(𝐤) and Hσ,R(𝐤) for the central scattering region and the right electrode, as well as the chemical potential μR, as follows.

Hσ,C(𝐤) = λ⁢Hσ,C(𝐤,1)+(1−λ)⁢Hσ,C(𝐤,2),
Hσ,R(𝐤) = λ⁢Hσ,R(𝐤,1)+(1−λ)⁢Hσ,R(𝐤,2),
μR = λ⁢μR(1)+(1−λ)⁢μR(2),

Here, the superscripts 1 and 2 mean that the quantities were calculated or used in the SCF calculations at the bias voltages chosen in advance. To ensure interpolation accuracy, one should in general perform interpolation within the interval, and therefore λ should be set between 0 and 1.

In the Step 3 calculation, interpolation is carried out by adding the following keywords to the input file.

      NEGF.tran.interpolate         on               # default=off, on|off
      NEGF.tran.interpolate.file1  c1-negf-0.5.tranb
      NEGF.tran.interpolate.file2  c1-negf-1.0.tranb
      NEGF.tran.interpolate.coes    0.7 0.3          # default=1.0 0.0

For interpolation, set the keyword NEGF.tran.interpolate to on. In the above example, the files cl-negf-0.5.tranb and cl-negf-1.0.tranb specified by the keywords NEGF.tran.interpolate.file1 and NEGF.tran.interpolate.file2 store the calculation results under bias voltages of 0.5 V and 1.0 V, respectively. Since the weights 0.7 and 0.3 are specified by the keyword NEGF.tran.interpolate.coes, the transmission and current at V=0.7×0.5+0.3×1.0=0.65 [V] are calculated by this interpolation method.

A comparison between the full SCF calculation and the interpolation method for the current and transmission of a one-dimensional carbon chain is shown in Fig. 49 (a) and (b). In the interpolation calculation, SCF calculations were performed at three bias voltages, 0 V, 0.5 V, and 1.0 V, and the results at the other bias voltages were obtained by interpolation. For comparison, current values obtained from full SCF calculations without using the interpolation method are also shown. From Fig. 49, it can be confirmed that this simple interpolation method gives very accurate results for both the current and the transmission. Although the appropriate choice of bias voltages used in the SCF calculations for interpolation depends on the system, this result suggests that the present method is very useful for interpolating bias-voltage effects while maintaining computational accuracy.
