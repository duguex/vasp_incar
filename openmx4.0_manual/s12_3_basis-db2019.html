12.3 Optimized basis functions provided by Database Ver. 2019

Optimized PAO functions are publicly available as Database Ver. 2019 on the OpenMX website (http://www.openmx-square.org/). Since the published basis functions are generated by the orbital optimization scheme [44] and have also been sufficiently tested by a series of benchmark calculations, we recommend the use of this database as the first choice for general users. For most elements in Database Ver. 2019, three systems were selected as a training set of chemical environments, and for the selected systems the PAO functions were optimized on the basis of the variational principle by the orbital optimization method [44]. These sets of optimized basis functions are integrated using a combined method of subspace rotation and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, and are stored in a single PAO file. Since the optimized PAO functions are optimized for several different sets of chemical environments, the transferability of the optimized PAO functions is considered to be high. In fact, a series of benchmark calculations on the database website shows good agreement with the results of the corresponding all-electron calculations. Referring to these benchmark calculations, it should be possible to choose appropriate basis functions (the cutoff radius and the number of basis functions) for each element. The input files used in the benchmark calculations can also be obtained from the website, and they are expected to be useful for becoming familiar with OpenMX calculations.

The accuracy of Database Ver. 2019 has been validated by the delta factor [43]. The average delta factor for 71 elements is 1.774 meV/atom, and the standard deviation is 1.702 meV/atom, which demonstrates the high accuracy of Database Ver. 2019. Therefore, general users are recommended to use these optimized basis functions. For the calculation of the delta factor, please also refer to Section 64.

For the convenience of users, Tables 1 and 2 provide suitable choices of basis functions. For each pseudopotential, three choices, Quick, Standard, and Precise, are prepared in these tables. Quick enables fast calculations with reasonable accuracy. Standard is an appropriate choice for most users and balances accuracy and efficiency. Precise reaches converged results in most cases. These tables provide guidelines for selecting basis functions, but naturally, basis functions should be chosen appropriately according to the purpose.
