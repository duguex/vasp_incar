46.3 Step 2: NEGF calculation

A. Definition of left electrode | device | right electrode

The regions L0, C0, and R0 shown in Fig. 44 can be specified as follows.

The geometrical structure of the central region C0 is specified by the keywords Atoms.Number and Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates as follows.

     Atoms.Number        18
     <Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates
       1  C  3.000  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
        .....
      18  C 28.500  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
     Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>
The geometrical structure of the left electrode region L0 is specified by the keywords LeftLeadAtoms.Number and LeftLeadAtoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates as follows.

     LeftLeadAtoms.Number         3
     <LeftLeadAtoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates
       1  C -1.500  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
       2  C  0.000  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
       3  C  1.500  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
     LeftLeadAtoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>
The geometrical structure of the right electrode region R0 is specified by the keywords RightLeadAtoms.Number and RightLeadAtoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates as follows.

     RightLeadAtoms.Number        3
     <RightLeadAtoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates
       1  C 30.000  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
       2  C 31.500  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
       3  C 33.000  0.000  0.000  2.0 2.0
     RightLeadAtoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>

The example shown here corresponds to the carbon-chain case discussed above. The central region C0 consists of 18 carbon atoms, and the left and right regions L0 and R0 each consist of three carbon atoms, with all nearest-neighbor distances equal to 1.5 Å . By specifying the geometrical structures of the device region C0 and the electrodes L0 and R0, OpenMX constructs the extended central region C(≡L0⁢|C0|⁢R0) shown in Fig. 44. To take into account relaxation of the electronic structure near the boundaries between the central region C0 and the outside L0⁢(R0), the Green function of the extended central region C is determined self-consistently. In order to calculate the Green function of the extended central region C using the NEGF method, the calculation model must be constructed so that the following two conditions are satisfied [81].

  1. 1.

    The localized basis orbitals ϕ in the region C0 overlap with the localized basis orbitals in the regions L0 and R0, but do not overlap with those in the regions L1 and R1.

  2. 2.

    The localized basis orbitals ϕ in the Li (Ri) region do not overlap with basis orbitals in cells farther away than the nearest neighboring cells Li−1 (Ri−1) and Li+1 (Ri+1).

Since the basis functions of OpenMX are strictly localized in real space [44, 45], by assigning localized orbitals with appropriate cutoff radii to each region, the above two conditions can always be satisfied by adjusting the unit-cell sizes of Li and Ri. It is not necessary to specify the unit cells of L0, C0, and R0, but note that periodicity is implicitly assumed. The semi-infinite electrodes are automatically constructed using the unit cells employed in the Step 1 band-structure calculation. The necessary information for this is stored in the file NEGF.filename.hks. Also, for the configuration shown in Fig. 44, the unit-cell vectors of the left and right electrodes within the bc plane must be identical. It is also implicitly assumed that the unit-cell vectors within the bc plane for the extended central region C are the same as those of the electrode regions. Within these constraints, the user can define the geometry.

The unit used to specify atomic positions is given as follows. 

    Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates.Unit   Ang # Ang|AU

However, in NEGF calculations only Ang or AU are supported. Please note that the FRAC specification is not available.

To check how OpenMX has interpreted the specified geometry, that information is printed in the standard output. For the carbon-chain example given above, the following information is output.

   <TRAN_Calc_GridBound>

   *******************************************************
   The extended cell consists of Left0-Center-Right0.
   The cells of left and right reads are connected as.
   ...|Left2|Left1|Left0-Center-Right0|Right1|Right2...

   Each atom in the extended cell is assigned as follows:
   where ’12’ and ’2’ mean that they are in ’Left0’, and
   ’12’ has overlap with atoms in the Left1,
   and ’13’ and ’3’ mean that they are in ’Right0’, and
   ’13’ has overlap with atoms in the ’Right1’, and also
   ’1’ means atom in the ’Center’.
   ********************************************************

   Atom1  = 12 Atom2  = 12 Atom3  = 12 Atom4  =  1 Atom5  =  1 Atom6  =  1 Atom7  =  1
   Atom8  =  1 Atom9  =  1 Atom10 =  1 Atom11 =  1 Atom12 =  1 Atom13 =  1 Atom14 =  1
   Atom15 =  1 Atom16 =  1 Atom17 =  1 Atom18 =  1 Atom19 =  1 Atom20 =  1 Atom21 =  1
   Atom22 = 13 Atom23 = 13 Atom24 = 13

Numerical labels are assigned to atoms in the extended central region consisting of L0⁢|C0|⁢R0. 12 and 2 mean that the atom belongs to L0, and 12 means that the basis functions overlap with atoms in L1. 13 and 3 mean that the atom belongs to R0, and 13 means that the basis functions overlap with atoms in R1. Also, 1 means that the atom belongs to C0. By checking this output, one can verify whether the geometrical structure of the system has been constructed properly.

B. Keywords

To perform the Step 2 NEGF calculation, the keyword scf.EigenvalueSolver should be set as follows.

    scf.EigenvalueSolver       NEGF

The keywords relevant to the NEGF calculation are listed below.

    NEGF.filename.hks.l     lead-chain.hks
    NEGF.filename.hks.r     lead-chain.hks

    NEGF.Num.Poles             100       # default=150
    NEGF.scf.Kgrid             1 1       # default=1 1

    NEGF.bias.voltage          0.0       # default=0.0 (eV)
    NEGF.bias.neq.im.energy    0.01      # default=0.01 (eV)
    NEGF.bias.neq.energy.step  0.02      # default=0.02 (eV)

The meaning of each keyword is explained below.

    NEGF.filename.hks.l     lead-chain.hks
    NEGF.filename.hks.r     lead-chain.hks

Files containing information on the electrode parts can be specified by the above two keywords. NEGF.filename.hks.l corresponds to the left electrode, and NEGF.filename.hks.r corresponds to the right electrode.

    NEGF.Num.Poles             100       # default=150

The equilibrium density matrix is evaluated by the contour integration method [81, 82]. The number of poles used in this method is specified by the keyword NEGF.Num.Poles.

    NEGF.scf.Kgrid             1 1       # default=1 1

The numbers of k points used to discretize the reciprocal lattice vectors 𝐛~ and 𝐜~ are specified by the keyword NEGF.scf.Kgrid. Since there is no periodicity along the a axis, no specification is required for the a direction.

    NEGF.scf.Iter.Band          6        # default=6

It has been found that, during the first few steps of the SCF calculation, assuming periodicity also along the a axis, in addition to the b and c axes, and using the conventional diagonalization method accelerates the final convergence. The difficulty of SCF convergence, which is often a serious issue in self-consistent NEGF calculations, is considerably alleviated by this method. The number of SCF steps using the conventional diagonalization method is specified by the keyword NEGF.scf.Iter.Band. Up to the number of SCF steps specified by NEGF.scf.Iter.Band, the conventional diagonalization method is used, and from the subsequent steps onward the NEGF method is applied. The default value is 6.

    NEGF.bias.voltage          0.0       # default=0.0 (eV)

The source-drain bias voltage applied between the left and right electrodes is specified in units of eV by the keyword NEGF.bias.voltage. This unit of eV corresponds to volts (V) when interpreted as an applied voltage. Since only the potential difference between the source and drain has physical meaning, the source-drain bias voltage, which is the difference in potential, is supplied through this keyword.

    NEGF.bias.neq.im.energy    0.01      # default=0.01 (eV)
    NEGF.bias.neq.energy.step  0.02      # default=0.02 (eV)

When a finite source-drain bias voltage is applied, part of the density matrix is calculated from the non-equilibrium Green function. Since the non-equilibrium Green function is, in general, not analytic on the complex plane, the contour integration method used for the equilibrium Green function cannot be applied. In the current implementation, the non-equilibrium Green function is evaluated on the real axis with a small imaginary part using a simple rectangular quadrature. The imaginary part is specified by the keyword NEGF.bias.neq.im.energy. The step width is given in units of eV by the keyword NEGF.bias.neq.energy.step. In most cases the default values provide sufficient accuracy, but for a detailed discussion of convergence see Ref. [81]. The number of energy points on the real axis used to evaluate the non-equilibrium Green function can be confirmed from the standard output and the file System.Name.out. In the example NEGF-Chain.dat, when the bias voltage is 0.5 V, 120 energy points are assigned, as can be confirmed from the following standard output.

   Intrinsic chemical potential (eV) of the leads
     Left lead:  -7.752843837400
     Right lead: -7.752843837400
     add voltage =  0.0000 (eV) to the  left lead: new ChemP (eV):  -7.7528
     add voltage =  0.5000 (eV) to the right lead: new ChemP (eV):  -7.2528

   Parameters for the integration of the non-equilibrium part
     lower bound:           -8.706843837400 (eV)
     upper bound:           -6.298843837400 (eV)
     energy step:            0.020000000000 (eV)
     number of steps:           120

The total number of energy points at which the Green function is evaluated is given by the sum of the number of real-axis energy points used for the non-equilibrium Green function and the number of poles used for the equilibrium Green function. Note that the computational time is proportional to the total number of energy points.

    NEGF.Poisson.Solver       FD     # FD|FFT, default=FD

In the NEGF method, the electrostatic potential for the difference electron density is evaluated either by the two-dimensional FFT + one-dimensional finite-difference method (FD) [81] or by the three-dimensional FFT method (FFT) [84]. The choice of this Poisson solver is made by the keyword NEGF.Poisson.Solver. For non-polar systems, both methods provide similar electrostatic potentials, whereas for polar systems the difference can become significant. In the former FD method, the boundary conditions between the electrode parts and the central region are satisfied exactly, and it is therefore theoretically the more appropriate method. The boundary condition here means that the electrostatic potential at the interface between the electrode parts and the central region is identical to the potential value obtained in the Step 1 calculation. If this boundary condition or the treatment of the charge density near the boundary is inappropriate, it may induce spurious scattering [81]. From the viewpoint of SCF convergence, the FD method often converges less easily, whereas the latter FFT method tends to show easier SCF convergence. The default is FD.

C. SCF convergence criterion

In the NEGF method, the SCF convergence criterion specified by the keyword scf.criterion is applied to the residual norm NormRD. In calculations other than NEGF, the SCF convergence criterion is applied to dUele.

D. Gate bias voltage

In the implementation of OpenMX Ver. 4.0, a gate voltage Vg⁢(x) is treated by adding the potential defined by the following expression.

Vg⁢(x)=Vg(0)⁢exp⁡[−(x−xcd)8],

Here, Vg(0) is a constant corresponding to the gate voltage, and can be specified by the keyword NEGF.gate.voltage.

      NEGF.gate.voltage   1.0    # default=0.0 (in eV)

Also, xc is the center position of the region C0, and d is the length of the cell vector of the region C0 along the a axis. As indicated by the form of the equation, the applied gate voltage acts mainly on the region C0 inside the extended central region C. This potential resembles the shape of the potential generated by image charges [85].

E. Density of states (DOS)

In an NEGF calculation, the density of states (DOS) can be calculated by setting the following keywords.

    Dos.fileout                 on              # on|off, default=off
    NEGF.Dos.energyrange     -15.0 25.0 5.0e-3  #default=-10.0 10.0 5.0e-3 (eV)
    NEGF.Dos.energy.div        200              # default=200
    NEGF.Dos.Kgrid             1 1              # default=1 1

To calculate the DOS in the NEGF method, set Dos.fileout to on. The energy range for the DOS calculation is given by the keyword NEGF.Dos.energyrange. Here, the first and second numbers are the lower and upper limits of the energy, and the third number is the imaginary value used to smooth the DOS. The energy range specified by NEGF.Dos.energyrange is divided into the number of points specified by the keyword NEGF.Dos.energy.div, and the DOS is calculated at those energy points. The respective numbers of k points used to discretize the reciprocal lattice vectors 𝐛~ and 𝐜~ are specified by the keyword NEGF.Dos.Kgrid. The accuracy can be improved by using larger values for NEGF.Dos.Kgrid than those used for NEGF.scf.Kgrid. After the NEGF calculation finishes, two files, System.Name.Dos.val and System.Name.Dos.vec, are generated. The subsequent analysis procedure is the same as in the general case. Also note that the zero of energy is set to the chemical potential of the left electrode.
