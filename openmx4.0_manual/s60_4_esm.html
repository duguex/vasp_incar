60.4 Combined use with the ESM method

In XPS calculations for surface systems and two-dimensional systems, it is necessary to insert a sufficiently large vacuum region along the direction normal to the slab surface in order to make the interaction between slabs sufficiently small. However, this convergence is generally slow, and from the viewpoints of computational cost and required memory, this approach is not always desirable. Instead of enlarging the vacuum region, the interaction between slabs can be removed by using the ESM method (Section 49). The ESM method is applied only to the initial-state electron density, while the exact Coulomb cutoff method (for gapped systems) is applied to the difference charge density.

Table 14 shows the convergence behavior of the N-1⁢s binding energy Eb of the two-dimensional material NBe2 calculated by the present method. Without ESM, Eb converges slowly with respect to the 𝐚-axis length, whereas with ESM it is already converged even for the minimum value of 10 Å, indicating that the combined use with the ESM method is effective. The input files for this calculation are N1Be2_IS.dat and N1Be2_FS.dat in the directory work. Here, the keywords for the ESM method are set as follows.

   ESM.switch             on1      # off, on1=v|v|v, on2=m|v|m, on3=v|v|m, on4=on2+EF
   ESM.buffer.range       4.5      # default=10.0 (ang),
   ESM.direction          z

Since NBe2 is a semiconductor, the final state is calculated as a charged state with scf.system.charge=1.0. On the other hand, since the ESM method is applied only to the initial-state electron density, on1 is used for ESM.switch. ESM.switch=on1 is a setting to be applied when the total charge of the system is neutral, and in the present calculation this setting is used because the initial-state electron density to which the ESM method is applied is neutral.
