60.3 Bulk Systems

Here, we explain how to calculate the absolute binding energy of a core level in a bulk system by taking TiC crystal as an example.

The calculation of the initial state is performed as follows.

    % mpirun -np 112 ./openmx TiC216.dat | tee TiC216.std

Here, no XPS-specific keyword is specified, but scf.SpinPolarization=on is used to perform a spin-polarized calculation. The spin-polarized calculation here is carried out to ensure consistency with the final-state calculation. However, the calculation result is nonmagnetic. The input file TiC216.dat used here is located in the directory work.

The final-state calculation is performed as follows.

    % mpirun -np 112 ./openmx TiC216-CH3.dat | tee TiC216-CH3.std

The input file TiC216-CH3.dat used here is located in the directory work. In this input file, the atomic species are defined as follows.

   <Definition.of.Atomic.Species
     Ti  Ti7.0-s3p2d2        Ti_PBE19
     C   C6.0_1s-s3p2d1      C_PBE19_1s
     C1  C6.0_1s_CH-s3p2d1   C_PBE19_1s
   Definition.of.Atomic.Species>

Then, the atomic species C1 is assigned to atom 5 as follows.

   Atoms.Number         216
   Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates.Unit   Ang # Ang|AU
   <Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates
      1  Ti   0.000000000000  0.000000000000  0.000000000000   6.0 6.0
      2  Ti   2.163500000000  2.163500000000  0.000000000000   6.0 6.0
      3  Ti   0.000000000000  2.163500000000  2.163500000000   6.0 6.0
      4  Ti   2.163500000000  0.000000000000  2.163500000000   6.0 6.0
      5  C1   2.163500000000  0.000000000000  0.000000000000   3.0 3.0
      6  C    0.000000000000  2.163500000000  0.000000000000   3.0 3.0
      7  C    0.000000000000  0.000000000000  2.163500000000   3.0 3.0
      8  C    2.163500000000  2.163500000000  2.163500000000   3.0 3.0
      ....
      ..
   Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>

By specifying the following keywords, a core hole is created in the 1⁢s state of atom 5. 

    scf.restart                 on
    scf.restart.filename        TiC216
    scf.coulomb.cutoff          on
    scf.core.hole               on
    scf.system.charge          0.0       # default=0.0

    <core.hole.state
      5 s 1
    core.hole.state>

In the final-state calculation, the Hartree potential VH consists of two contributions [97]. They are the periodic part VH(P) and the nonperiodic part VH(NP) defined by

VH⁢(𝐫)=VH(P)⁢(𝐫)+VH(NP)⁢(𝐫), (68)

The periodic part VH(P) is calculated from the charge density obtained in the initial-state calculation through the Poisson equation under periodic boundary conditions. The charge density obtained in the initial-state calculation is specified by the keywords scf.restart and scf.restart.filename. The nonperiodic part VH(NP) is calculated for the difference charge density Δ⁢ρ⁢(𝐫)=ρf⁢(𝐫)−ρi⁢(𝐫) by using the exact Coulomb cutoff method [101]. Here, ρf⁢(𝐫) and ρi⁢(𝐫) are the charge densities of the final and initial states, respectively. The cutoff radius of the Coulomb interaction is set to half the length of the shortest lattice vector. To activate the exact Coulomb cutoff method, set the keyword scf.coulomb.cutoff to on. The core hole is created by the keywords scf.core.hole and core.hole.state. In the present case, a core hole is created in the 1s state of atom 5. Since TiC crystal is metallic, note that the keyword scf.system.charge is set to 0.0. When treating a gapped system, scf.system.charge must be set to 1.

After completing the initial-state and final-state calculations, the total energies are obtained from the output files.

     Initial state: -10499.900104007471 (Hartree)
     Final state:   -10489.553360141708 (Hartree)

Then, using Eq. (67), the binding energy can be calculated as follows.

Eb(metal) = Ef(0)⁢(N)−Ei(0)⁢(N),
= −10489.553360141708−(−10499.900104007471)=10.346743865763⁢(Hartree),
≈ 281.55⁢(eV).

The obtained value, 281.55 eV, is found to agree well with the experimental value of 281.5 eV [102].

As an example of a gapped system, let us explain the calculation for crystalline silicon. The initial-state and final-state calculations are performed as follows.

    % mpirun -np 256 ./openmx Si-4-SOI.dat | tee Si-4-SOI.std
    % mpirun -np 256 ./openmx Si-4-CH-SOI1.dat | tee Si-4-CH-SOI1.std
    % mpirun -np 256 ./openmx Si-4-CH-SOI6.dat | tee Si-4-CH-SOI6.std

Si-4-SOI.dat is the input file for the initial-state calculation, while Si-4-CH-SOI1.dat and Si-4-CH-SOI6.dat are the input files for the final-state calculations, in which holes are introduced into the core levels specified by 2⁢p3/2⁢(J=3/2,M=3/2) and 2⁢p1/2⁢(J=1/2,M=−1/2), respectively. To take the spin-orbit interaction of the 2⁢p state of Si atoms into account, specify the following keywords and perform a noncollinear calculation. 

    scf.SpinPolarization        nc         # On|Off|NC
    scf.SpinOrbit.Coupling      on         # On|Off, default=off

After completing the initial-state and final-state calculations, the total energies are obtained from the output files.

     Initial state:          -34820.483255130872 (Hartree)
     Final state for SOI1:   -34816.628201335407 (Hartree)
     Final state for SOI6:   -34816.601864921540 (Hartree)

The chemical potential is obtained from the initial-state calculation. Using Eq. (66), the binding energies of Si-4-CH-SOI1.dat and Si-4-CH-SOI6.dat can be calculated as follows.

For⁢2⁢p3/2
Eb(bulk) = Ef(0)⁢(N−1)−Ei(0)⁢(N)+μ0,
= −34816.628201335−(−34820.483255131)−0.201641583=3.65341221⁢(Hartree),
≈ 99.41⁢(eV),
For⁢2⁢p1/2
Eb(bulk) = Ef(0)⁢(N−1)−Ei(0)⁢(N)+μ0,
= −34816.601864922−(−34820.483255131)−0.201641583=3.6797486⁢(Hartree),
≈ 100.13⁢(eV).

The binding energies 99.41 eV and 100.13 eV for 2⁢p3/2 and 2⁢p1/2, respectively, show good agreement with the experimental values of 99.2 eV and 99.8 eV [102]. Here, as listed in Table 13, the degeneracies of 2⁢p3/2 and 2⁢p1/2 are 4 and 2, respectively. In gapped systems, the convergence of the absolute binding energy with respect to the unit-cell size is slow. Figures 92(a) and (b) show the relative binding energies in gapped systems and metals, respectively, as functions of the distance between core holes. These results indicate that convergence is slow in gapped systems and that a large supercell is required to obtain a converged value. On the other hand, in metals, the binding energy converges rapidly even at short core-hole distances. Figure 92(c) shows the difference charge density in crystalline silicon induced by the creation of a core hole in the 2⁢p state. In order to screen the potential generated by the core hole, charge redistribution occurs over a range of approximately 7 Å from the core hole. The slow convergence observed in gapped systems is caused by this long-range charge redistribution. By contrast, in metals the charge redistribution is confined to a relatively narrow region, and therefore rapid convergence is achieved, as shown in Fig. 92(b).

Other calculation examples and the input files used in the calculations are available at the website
https://www.openmx-square.org/vps_pao_core2019/ for reference. In addition, applications of the present method to silicon, borophene, and Pt single atoms dispersed on graphene are discussed in Refs. [103, 104, 105].
