39 Orbital magnetic moment

The orbital magnetic moment at each atomic site is calculated by default in non-collinear DFT calculations. Since the local orbital magnetic moment appears as a consequence of spin-orbit interaction, this value becomes finite only when spin-orbit interaction is taken into account [129, 130]. As an example, we explain the non-collinear LDA+U calculation (U=5eV) for bulk iron oxide using the input file FeO_NC.dat in the work directory. For LDA+U calculations, see Section 40. The calculated orbital magnetic moments and spin magnetic moments at the Fe sites are shown in Table 7. In OpenMX, the expectation value of the orbital angular momentum at each atomic site is evaluated on the basis of local basis functions, and not only the total orbital magnetic moment but also the contributions decomposed into s, p, d, and f orbitals and each multiplicity are output. Therefore, it is possible to analyze which localized orbitals are the main origin of the orbital magnetic moment. In general, in 3⁢d transition metals the orbital magnetic moment is partially quenched by the crystal field, but a finite value can be recovered when spin-orbit interaction and orbital polarization by DFT+U are taken into account. The orbital magnetic moments decomposed into orbital components are output in System.Name.out as follows. Here System.Name means the keyword System.Name.

As shown in Table 7, the OpenMX results are consistent with other calculations for the spin and orbital magnetic moments of a series of 3⁢d transition-metal oxides. However, the absolute value of the orbital magnetic moment is strongly affected by computational conditions such as the basis functions and the onsite U in the DFT+U method. It should also be noted that rather large basis functions including polarization functions are necessary for converged calculations of orbital magnetic moments. For quantitative comparison, we recommend interpreting the results together with the spin magnetic moments only after sufficiently converging the basis functions, U, the number of k points, and the electronic temperature. On the other hand, it is empirically known that the spin magnetic moment is relatively insensitive to computational conditions.

Here, the orbital magnetic moment output by OpenMX (OpenMX convention) has the opposite sign to the physical orbital magnetic moment of the electron. Similarly, the spin magnetic moment output by OpenMX (OpenMX convention) also has the opposite sign to the physical spin magnetic moment of the electron. For the spin magnetic moment, see also Sec. 37 'Non-collinear DFT'.
