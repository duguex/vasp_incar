47.1.4 Molecular-orbital guiding functions

In general, a unit cell of a molecular crystal contains many atoms, and therefore the number of valence bands is also large. The CWF method can generate Wannier functions in a numerically stable manner even in such cases, but it is not always easy to obtain a clear physical interpretation. On the other hand, near the Fermi energy, the band structure of a molecular crystal is often composed of only a few bands. Therefore, OpenMX Ver. 4.0 provides a method to generate Wannier functions for a small number of bands using molecular-like orbitals as guiding functions. Here, the procedure is explained using Rb3C60 and TTF-TCNQ (tetrathiafulvalene-tetracyanoquinodimethane) as examples. First, run the calculation for Rb3C60 as follows.

     % mpirun -np 28 openmx Rb3C60-CWF3.dat | tee Rb3C60-CWF3.std

If the calculation finishes normally, Rb3C60-CWF3.Band and Rb3C60-CWF3.CWF.Band are output. By following the procedure in Sec. 22, “Band dispersion”, the band dispersion shown in Fig. 56(a) is obtained. It can be seen that the Wannier-interpolated bands reproduce the original bands near the Fermi energy well. Figure 56(c) shows the obtained CWFs. One can clearly see that they are Wannier functions of molecular-like orbitals localized on the C60 molecule. Figure 56(b) shows the tight-binding parameter t calculated from the CWFs as a function of distance. Since t is short ranged, the locality of the CWFs is confirmed. This figure was obtained by plotting Rb3C60-CWF3.CWF.Dis_vs_H.


We now explain the procedure for generating molecular-like CWFs step by step. As can be seen in Fig. 56(a), there are three bands near the Fermi energy. Since we would like to generate Wannier functions for these three bands, the polynomial window function is set as follows.

   CWF.window.function               Poly
   CWF.disentangling.Erange     -0.6 -0.5 0.5 0.6

With this setting, only the three bands are taken into account in the CWF calculation. Next, in order to generate molecular-like orbitals as guiding functions, atoms are grouped by the following keyword. 

   <CWF.MO.Grouped.Atoms
     1 1
     2 1
     3 1
     ...
     ...
     ...
    58 1
    59 1
    60 1
    61 2
    62 3
    63 4
   CWF.MO.Grouped.Atoms>

The first column is the serial number of the atom, corresponding to the first column of the keyword Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates. The second column is the group number. In this example, atoms 1 to 60 belong to group 1, atom 61 belongs to group 2, atom 62 to group 3, and atom 63 to group 4. A density matrix is constructed for each group, and molecular-like orbitals are calculated from its singular value decomposition [133]. The polynomial window function is taken into account in the calculation of the density matrix, and in this example only contributions from the three bands near the Fermi energy are included. It is not obvious how many molecular-like orbitals from each group should be used as guiding functions, but this can be determined by examining Rb3C60-CWF3.CWF_MOs_info. In Rb3C60-CWF3.CWF_MOs_info, the occupations of the molecular-like orbitals are listed as follows.

   # Group index= 1
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1     3.772763223603     0.971280428260
    2     3.772763158788     0.971280364811
    3     3.772825091566     0.971169938918
    4     5.104541857210     0.281333604312
    ...
   # Group index= 2
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1    -0.206973824904     0.005047865655
    ...
   # Group index= 3
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1    -0.208992494726     0.005047397816
    ...
   # Group index= 4
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1     0.091644733298     0.009311699976
    ...

Thus, the occupation is dominated by molecular-like orbitals in group 1, while the molecular-like orbitals in groups 2, 3, and 4 (strictly speaking, since each of groups 2, 3, and 4 contains only one atom, calling them molecular-like orbitals is somewhat abusive) are almost unoccupied. Therefore, the guiding functions are specified as follows. 

   <CWF.MO.Selection
    1  3
    2  0
    3  0
    4  0
   CWF.MO.Selection>

Three molecular-like orbitals are taken from group 1, and zero from the others. Note that the number of guiding functions matches the number of bands of interest. If the number of bands is set smaller than the number of guiding functions, the calculation becomes ill-defined because of the indeterminacy of the singular-value problem. By selecting the three bands near the Fermi energy with the polynomial window function, and choosing the three molecular-like orbitals with the largest occupations as guiding functions, highly accurate Wannier interpolation is achieved. The obtained CWFs are molecular-like Wannier functions localized on the C60 molecule, giving the intended result. On the Web [134], calculation examples for Rb3C60 using PAOs as guiding functions are also available, and may be useful as references.

As a second example, run the calculation for TTF-TCNQ as follows.

     % mpirun -np 28 openmx TTF-TCNQ-3.dat | tee TTF-TCNQ-3.std

If the calculation finishes normally, TTF-TCNQ-3.Band and TTF-TCNQ-3.CWF.Band are output. By following the procedure in Sec. 22, “Band dispersion”, the band dispersion shown in Fig. 57(a) is obtained. It can be seen that the Wannier-interpolated bands reproduce the original bands near the Fermi energy well. The inset in Fig. 57(b) shows the obtained CWFs. One can see that they are Wannier functions of molecular-like orbitals localized on either the TTF molecule or the TCNQ molecule. Figure 57(b) also shows the tight-binding parameter t calculated from the CWFs as a function of distance. Since t is short ranged, the locality of the CWFs is confirmed. This figure was obtained by plotting TTF-TCNQ-3.CWF.Dis_vs_H. Although the unit cell contains two TTF molecules and two TCNQ molecules, each molecule was grouped separately to calculate molecular-like orbitals. In TTF-TCNQ-3.CWF_MOs_info, the occupations of the molecular-like orbitals are listed as follows.

   # Group index= 1
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1     2.871502832962     1.275542066141
    2     3.046041661701     0.115327342758
    ...
   # Group index= 2
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1     2.871502892990     1.275542046191
    2     3.046041703156     0.115327331814
    ...
   # Group index= 3
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1     3.278477581295     1.077497609731
    2     3.665521186670     0.384150232883
    ...
   # Group index= 4
   # 1st column: serial number
   # 2nd column: on-site energy (eV) relative to chemical potential
   # 3rd column: population
    1     3.278477549847     1.077497606232
    2     3.665521083002     0.384150244280
    ...

It can be seen that, in each group, the first molecular-like orbital is the dominant one. Therefore, one molecular-like orbital from each group was selected and used as a guiding function. For details of the keyword settings, please refer to TTF-TCNQ-3.dat in the work directory.
