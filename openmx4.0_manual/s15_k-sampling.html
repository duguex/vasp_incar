15 k-point sampling

This section is for users who want to choose an appropriate k-point mesh for periodic calculations. First understand the difference between GCenter and Regular, and then decide which sampling is appropriate for your system and symmetry. If you are still learning the basic input format, it is easier to read this section together with Section 8.

OpenMX Ver. 4.0 provides two types of k-point sampling methods (GCenter and Regular), which can be selected using the following keyword: 

  scf.Generation.Kpoint    GCenter   # GCenter|Regular, default=GCenter

The default setting is GCenter. GCenter is a Γ-centered k-point sampling method, and always includes the Γ point regardless of whether the number of k-points is odd or even. Based on the number of divisions specified along each reciprocal lattice vector, k-points are placed symmetrically in the Brillouin zone around the Γ point. This is a standard sampling method and is widely used for insulators, semiconductors, molecules, surfaces, interfaces, and supercell calculations including defects. Because the Γ point is explicitly included, this method is convenient when the dispersion and wavefunction contributions near the Γ point are important. It also has the advantage that 𝐤 and −𝐤 are treated on an equal footing. In addition to the Γ point, the k-points are constructed so as to always include Time-Reversal Invariant Momentum (TRIM) points. A TRIM point is a special k-point that satisfies

𝐤=−𝐤+𝐆 (1)

for a reciprocal lattice vector 𝐆. The Γ point is also a TRIM point because it satisfies this condition, while TRIM points other than Γ lie on the boundary of the first Brillouin zone. Therefore, this method is well suited for systems in which the dispersion and wavefunction contributions on the zone boundary are important. For ordinary purposes, the default choice GCenter is recommended.

Regular is also a k-point sampling method centered around the Γ point, and the k-points are generated by

𝐤l⁢m⁢n = q1⁢l⁢a~1+q2⁢m⁢a~2+q3⁢n⁢a~3,
qi⁢l = −12+2⁢l+12⁢Nii=1,2,3,l=0∼Ni−1, (2)

where a~i are reciprocal lattice vectors, and Ni are the numbers of k-points specified by the keyword scf.Kgrid. The Γ point is included only when all {Ni} are odd. Note also that k-points on the boundary of the first Brillouin zone are not included.

The numbers of discrete points along the reciprocal lattice vector directions that define the first Brillouin zone are specified by the following keyword.

     scf.Kgrid    9 7 8

In this case, uniform meshes of 9, 7, and 8 divisions are generated along the reciprocal lattice vectors 𝐚~, 𝐛~, and 𝐜~, respectively, depending on the choice of GCenter or Regular.
