62.4 Benchmark Calculations

Several benchmark calculations are shown below.

Si
The real part of the dielectric function of crystalline silicon is shown in Fig. 94. To examine the dependence of the dielectric function on the number of k points, calculations were carried out systematically by changing the k-point mesh. As the number of k points is increased from 10×10×10 to 100×100×100, the real part of the dielectric function converges. It is found that a fine k-point grid is required to obtain a sufficiently converged result. Tables 16 and 17 show the computational time and parallel efficiency for the optical conductivity and dielectric function of crystalline silicon. These results indicate that, if 1000 CPU cores are available, calculations for systems containing 1000 atoms are feasible.



β-PVDF
The real part of the dielectric function of β-PVDF (polyvinylidene fluoride) is shown in Fig. 95. To examine the dependence of the dielectric function on the number of k points, calculations were carried out systematically by changing the k-point mesh. It is found that a 6×9×21 k-point mesh is required to obtain a converged solution. Tables 18 and 19 show the computational time and parallel efficiency for the optical conductivity and dielectric function of β-PVDF. The parallel efficiency is good, and it has been confirmed that the elapsed time is less than one hour when 256 CPU cores are used. For reference, Fig. 96 also shows the x⁢x, y⁢y, and z⁢z components of the real part of the dielectric function of β-PVDF.



R-phase VO2
The real part of the dielectric function of VO2 (R phase) is shown in Fig. 97. To examine the dependence of the dielectric function on the number of k points, calculations were carried out systematically by changing the k-point mesh. It is found that a 16×16×16 k-point mesh is required to obtain a converged solution. Tables 20 and 21 show the computational time and parallel efficiency for the conductivity and dielectric function of VO2 (R phase). The parallel efficiency is good, and large-scale calculations are achieved with an elapsed time of about one hour.
