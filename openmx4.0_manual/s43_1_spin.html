43.1 Zeeman term for spin magnetic moments

The following keywords introduce the Zeeman term for spin magnetic moments as an interaction with a uniform magnetic field.

      scf.NC.Zeeman.Spin           on        # on|off, default=off
      scf.NC.Mag.Field.Spin       100.0      # default=0.0 (Tesla)

When introducing the Zeeman term for spin magnetic moments, set the keyword scf.NC.Zeeman.Spin to on. The strength of the uniform magnetic field is specified in Tesla by the keyword scf.NC.Mag.Field.Spin. This method can be used as a constraint DFT method, and the direction of the magnetic field can be specified for each atom. The direction of the magnetic field acting on the spin magnetic moment is specified by the keyword Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates as follows.

  <Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates
   1  Sc  0.000  0.000  0.000   6.6 4.4  10.0 50.0  160.0 20.0  1  on
   2  Sc  2.000  0.000  0.000   6.6 4.4  80.0 50.0  160.0 20.0  1  on
  Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>

To specify the direction of the magnetic field acting on spin magnetic moments, the Euler angles (θ, ϕ) are given in the 8th and 9th columns. The 12th column is the switch for the constraint condition. 1 means with constraint, and 0 means without constraint. Since the direction of the applied magnetic field can be controlled for each atomic site, this method provides a way to study various non-collinear spin structures. Note, however, that the keywords scf.NC.Zeeman.Spin and scf.Constraint.NC.Spin are incompatible with each other. Therefore, when scf.NC.Zeeman.Spin is on, the keyword scf.Constraint.NC.Spin must be set to off as follows.

      scf.Constraint.NC.Spin       off       # on|off, default=off

The Zeeman term for the direction of spin magnetic moments explained in this section and the constraint method described in the previous section are both ways to control spin orientation, but note that when the Zeeman term is added, the magnitude of the spin magnetic moment may increase. Also, since the obtained total energy includes the Zeeman energy itself, when it is compared directly with the energy landscape at zero field, the presence or absence of the Zeeman term should be distinguished carefully in the interpretation.
