47.2.3 Checking the optimization process of the spread function

The optimization process is written to standard output. Please check the optimization progress by the following methods. For convenience, we assume here that the standard output is saved in the file stdout.std. The examples shown below can be reproduced by the user using the input file Si.dat in openmx*.*/work/wf_example.

DISE

The self-consistent loop of the disentangling treatment (the first stage of the optimization) can be checked as follows.

    % grep "DISE" stdout.std

    |  Iter  | Omega_I (Angs^2) | Delta_I (Angs^2) |  ---> DISE
    |     1  |   18.371525257652|   18.371525257652|  ---> DISE
    |     2  |   17.955767336391|   -0.415757921261|  ---> DISE
    |     3  |   17.659503060694|   -0.296264275698|  ---> DISE
    |     4  |   17.454033576174|   -0.205469484520|  ---> DISE
    |     5  |   17.311180447271|   -0.142853128902|  ---> DISE
    |     6  |   17.210945408916|   -0.100235038355|  ---> DISE
    |     7  |   17.139778800398|   -0.071166608519|  ---> DISE
    |     8  |   17.088603102826|   -0.051175697572|  ---> DISE
    |     9  |   17.051329329614|   -0.037273773211|  ---> DISE
    |    10  |   17.023842837298|   -0.027486492316|  ---> DISE
    ........
    .....
    ...
    .

Here, Iter means the iteration number, Omega_I the gauge-invariant part of the spread function, and Delta_I the difference in Omega_I between two neighboring steps. The convergence criterion given to the keyword Wannier.Dis.Conv.Criterion is applied to Delta_I.

CONV

The optimization process of the gauge-dependent part of the spread function (the second stage of the optimization) can be checked as follows.

  % grep "CONV" stdout.std

  Opt Step |Mode of Gradient|d_Omega_in_steps|     d_Omega   | (in Angs^2) ---> CONV
  | SD    1 | 6.52434844E-01 | 5.41612774E-04 |-5.41340331E-04|  ---> CONV
  | SD    2 | 6.51123660E-01 | 5.40524307E-04 |-5.40253165E-04|  ---> CONV
  .....
  .....
  | SD  200 | 4.77499752E-01 | 3.96392019E-04 |-3.96271308E-04|  ---> CONV
  |Opt Step |Mode of Gradient|     d_Omega    | (Angs^2) ---> CONV
  | CG    1 | 8.61043764E-01 | -3.24716990E-01|  ---> CONV
  .....
  .....
  | CG   58 | 1.67083857E-12 | -5.37225101E-13|  ---> CONV
  | CG   59 | 5.44431651E-13 | -1.98972260E-13|  ---> CONV
  ************************************************************* ---> CONV
  CONVERGENCE ACHIEVED !                      ---> CONV
  ************************************************************* ---> CONV
  CONVERGENCE ACHIEVED !                      ---> SPRD

Here, Opt Step is the optimization step in either the SD or CG method, and Mode of Gradient is the absolute value of the gradient of the spread function. In the SD method, the difference in the gauge-dependent spread function between two neighboring steps is evaluated in two different ways. The results are d_Omega_in_steps and d_Omega. d_Omega_in_steps is

d⁢Ω=ϵ⁢∑𝐤‖G(𝐤)‖2,

where ϵ is the step length and G(𝐤) is the gradient of the spread function. The details of this expression are explained in Ref. [135]. On the other hand, d_Omega is given by

d⁢Ω=Ω(n+1)−Ω(n),

where n is the iteration number. In the CG method, only d_Omega is evaluated. The convergence criterion given by the keyword Wannier.Minimizing.Conv.Criterion is applied to Mode of Gradient.

SPRD

The change in the spread of the Wannier functions can be checked as follows.

Here, Opt Step is the optimization step in either the SD or CG method. Omega_I is the gauge-invariant part of the spread function. Omega_D and Omega_OD are the contributions from the diagonal and off-diagonal gauge-dependent parts, respectively. Tot_Omega is the sum of these three components of the spread function.
