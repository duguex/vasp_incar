40.1 Standard settings

40.1.1 Choice of DFT+U method: simplified or generalized

The following two keywords are used to select the occupation-number operator, functional form, and double-counting term in the DFT+U method. 

  scf.Hubbard.U                 on  # on|off, default=off
  scf.DFTU.Type                 2   # 1:Simplified(Dudarev)|2:General, default=1

These keywords are common to both collinear and non-collinear calculations [22, 23]. scf.DFTU.Type=1 corresponds to the so-called simplified rotationally invariant form proposed by Dudarev et al. [26], where only U (Hubbard U) plays a role [21]. In the more general DFT+U method, not only U but also J (Hund’s exchange interaction J) is an input parameter. When using the DFT+U method, set the keyword scf.SpinPolarization to either on or nc.

The occupation-number operator [21] is specified by the following keyword .

  scf.Hubbard.Occupation        dual  # onsite|full|dual, default=dual

Among the three occupation-number operators (onsite, full, dual), only the dual operator satisfies the sum rule (the trace of the occupation-number matrix gives the total number of electrons). For details of the onsite, full, and dual operators, refer to Ref. [21].

If atomic species are defined by the keyword Definition.of.Atomic.Species as follows,

  <Definition.of.Atomic.Species
    Ni Ni6.0S-s2p2d2f1  Ni_CA19S
    O  O5.0-s2p2d1      O_CA19
  Definition.of.Atomic.Species>

the values of U and J for each orbital (in eV) are specified by the keyword Hubbard.U.values as follows. 

  <Hubbard.U.values   # eV
    Ni 1s 0.0 2s 0.0 1p 0.0 2p 0.0 1d 5.0 2d 0.0 1f 0.0
    O  1s 0.0 2s 0.0 1p 0.0 2p 0.0 1d 0.0
  Hubbard.U.values>

  <Hund.J.values      # eV
    Ni 1s 0.0 2s 0.0 1p 0.0 2p 0.0 1d 0.9 2d 0.0 1f 0.0
    O  1s 0.0 2s 0.0 1p 0.0 2p 0.0 1d 0.0
  Hund.J.values>

The specification of U (J) starts with <Hubbard.U.values (<Hund.J.values), and ends with Hubbard.U.values> (Hund.J.values>). Please provide U and J in eV for all basis functions in the above format. 1s and 2s mean the first s orbital and the second s orbital, respectively. The number after 1s is the value of U (or J) for the first s orbital. The same rule applies to the p and d orbitals. If scf.DFTU.Type=1, only the values of U are read, and in that case it is not necessary to specify the values of J.

40.1.2 Choice of the correction term for double counting

The correction for double counting (DC) is common to embedding methods including the DFT+U method. When scf.DFTU.Type=2 is used, the DC term must be specified by the following keyword.

  scf.dc.Type                 cFLL\t   # sFLL|sAMF|cFLL|cAMF, default=sFLL

In the above example, the cFLL DC term is selected. In the choices sFLL, sAMF, cFLL, and cAMF, c and s mean density functional theory based on charge (spin-unpolarized) density and spin density LDA/GGA, respectively. Meanwhile, FLL and AMF correspond to the fully localized limit (FLL) and around mean-field (AMF) treatments of the double-counting term. For detailed definitions and behaviors of each method, refer to Refs. [22, 23]. When the DC term cFLL or cAMF is selected, note that the spin-density exchange-correlation potential in LDA (or GGA) is not taken into account in the SCF loop [22, 23]. scf.dc.Type=sFLL corresponds to the form proposed by Liechtenstein et al. [27]. Also note that when the simplified rotationally invariant form (scf.DFTU.Type=1) is specified, the sFLL DC term is implicitly assumed. Which type of DFT+U method has been specified can be confirmed from the following messages in the standard output (before the start of the SCF loop).

  For scf.DFTU.Type=1,

  *******************************************************
                     DFT+U Type and DC
  *******************************************************
                scf.DFTU.Type: 1(Simplified)


  For scf.DFTU.Type=2 and scf.dc.Type=cFLL,

  *******************************************************
                     DFT+U Type and DC
  *******************************************************
       scf.DFTU.Type: 2(General)   scf.dc.Type: cFLL

By diagonalizing the occupation-number matrix at each atomic site, eigenvalues and eigenvectors are obtained. The eigenvalues of the occupation-number matrix physically correspond to electron occupations on localized orbitals. The eigenvectors correspond to those localized orbitals.

40.1.3 Orbital polarization

The DFT+U functional has multiple extrema in the degrees of freedom of orbital occupation, and the result of an SCF calculation may converge to a local extremum near the initial occupation numbers. Therefore, the converged electronic state is not necessarily the ground state with the lowest energy. When the self-interaction is sufficiently removed, occupations close to integers are favored over fractional occupations, and in partially occupied degenerate systems such as CoO, multiple local solutions corresponding to the presence or absence of orbital polarization can appear. To find a ground state with orbital polarization, it is possible to explicitly promote orbital polarization by the following flag. 

  For collinear calculations

  <Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates   # Unit=AU
    1 Ni 0.0     0.0     0.0     10.0 6.0  on
    2 Ni 3.94955 3.94955 0.0     6.0  10.0 on
    3 O  3.94955 0.0     0.0     3.0  3.0  on
    4 O  3.94955 3.94955 3.94955 3.0  3.0  on
  Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>

  For non-collinear calculations

  <Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates   # Unit=AU
    1 Ni 0.0     0.0     0.0     10.0 6.0  40.0 10.0 0 on
    2 Ni 3.94955 3.94955 0.0     6.0  10.0 40.0 10.0 0 on
    3 O  3.94955 0.0     0.0     3.0  3.0  10.0 40.0 0 on
    4 O  3.94955 3.94955 3.94955 3.0  3.0  10.0 40.0 0 on
  Atoms.SpeciesAndCoordinates>

When orbital polarization is to be promoted, specify on in the last column. For ordinary calculations, specify off. Since the promotion of orbital polarization is applied to each atomic site, on or off must be specified for all atoms. If no explicit specification is given, off is set. When on is specified, orbital polarization is promoted during the first few SCF steps, after which ordinary calculations are carried out in the subsequent SCF steps. This treatment promotes orbital polarization and often yields the ground state, but it may also lead convergence toward an unstable direction and is therefore not a universal method. For details, see Ref. [21]. The use of the orbital-polarization flag is also not recommended when scf.dc.Type=cFLL and cAMF are used.
